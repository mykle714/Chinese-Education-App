## 5. Bugs and inconsistencies found in the pipeline

Recorded rather than fixed, per §6.

### 5.1 `spanish/backfill-frequency-score.js` silently ignores `--words=`

Its row query (lines ~232-240) has **no word filter at all**:

```sql
SELECT id, word1, pronunciation, definitions
FROM dictionaryentries_es
WHERE language = 'es' AND discoverable = TRUE
  AND (("frequencyScore" IS NULL OR "difficulty" IS NULL)${isStale ? ` OR ${staleClause()}` : ''})
ORDER BY ...
```

`--words=` is parsed into `targetWords` at line 100 and then never used. Passing the planner's
`--words=` list therefore silently scopes to the **entire table** — a `--stale` run exported
1257 prompts when 8 were asked for. This is the one place in the run where the skill's
"pass `--words=` and let the script decide" contract is not honoured by the script.
Suggested fix: add `AND word1 = ANY($1)` when `targetWords` is set, mirroring the other es scripts.

### 5.2 The oracle answers file must be cumulative across a multi-stage chain

Not a bug, but non-obvious and worth documenting for the next runner. Every `apply` run
**replays the whole chain from the first prompt**, because nothing is written until an entry
completes. If you overwrite `oracle-answers.jsonl` with only the current stage's answers, the
run restarts at stage 1 and re-exports prompts you already answered. The answers file has to
accumulate. I ended up with a small append helper for this; the skill doc could say so
explicitly.

### 5.3 The clusterer reads `raw`, not the cleaned `definitions`

`spanish/backfill-cluster-definitions` clusters the **source** gloss blocks, so the comma
splitting that `process-definitions-array` performs is not reflected. `abrir` was split into
21 clean glosses by the definitions pass and then arrived at the clusterer with its original
13 unsplit source glosses (`"to open, open up"` etc.). The two scripts are operating on
different views of the same entry. Worth deciding deliberately which one is intended.

### 5.4 An "unchanged" row is not stamped by `process-definitions-array`

`abuelo` came back `unchanged` (critic confirmed the existing order, nothing to write) and was
still reported as pending by the planner on the next run, and again in a later round. Rows
whose correct outcome is "no change" appear to never get a version stamp, so they re-enter
every plan forever. Small, but it inflates every future plan.

## 6. Data-quality findings in `dictionaryentries_es`

These are source (Wiktionary import) problems surfaced by reading 881 entries closely. They
are the most actionable output of the run after the clusters themselves.

### 6.1 Entries with no usable definition at all

- **`venir`** — the worst entry in the corpus. Its only two "glosses" are Wiktionary *section
  headings*: `"Senses relating to literal movement. [+de (from) or desde (from), …]"` and
  `"Senses relating to figurative movement"`. The actual sub-senses were lost at import. One of
  the most frequent verbs in Spanish currently ships with no definition.
- **`estar`** — every gloss is `"to be"` plus a grammatical description. The ser/estar contrast
  is stated nowhere, in this entry or in `ser`'s.
- **`gustar`** — the lead gloss is a paragraph-long grammatical essay that cross-references
  Italian *piacere*, Romanian *plăcea*, German *gefallen* and Russian *нравиться*. Genuinely
  useful information, completely unusable as a card definition.

### 6.2 Glosses split mid-parenthetical during import

A recurring import defect: one source gloss became two or three array elements, orphaning the
tail. Confirmed on **`carretera`** (`…(“carter` / `cartwright”)`), **`cruce`** (twice),
**`persona`** (`person (an individual` / `usually a human being)`), **`despegar`** (unclosed
paren), **`odio`** (unclosed paren), **`matar`** (three fragments), **`ir`**, **`salir`**,
**`saber`**, **`tener`**, and **`poner`** (three separate splits in one entry).

### 6.3 Glosses that would teach a learner an error

- **`jugar`** → `"to play (an instrument)"`. Spanish uses **tocar** for instruments; *jugar un
  instrumento* is the classic English-speaker mistake. Shipping this actively teaches it.
- **`pariente`** → `"parents"`. *pariente* means **relative**; parents is *padres*.
- **`prestar`** → `"to borrow"`. *prestar* is to **lend**; borrowing is *pedir prestado*.
- **`aprender`** → `"to teach"`. Non-standard; *enseñar* is the verb.
- **`actor`** → `"defendant"`. In Spanish legal usage *actor* is the **plaintiff**.
- **`cobro`** → `"payment, paying"`. *cobro* is the receiving side; paying is *pago*.

### 6.4 Glosses with no plausible basis (likely import artefacts)

`bolígrafo` → "drunkard"; `luna` → "shirt" and "shield"; `mosquito` → "trimmer";
`regalar` → "to melt"; `señor` → "free" (adjective); `dedo` → "thimble" (that is *dedal*);
`escapar` → "to manage / to get by"; `cuerpo` → "field"; `hierro` → "importance";
`queso` → "foot" (unglossed slang); `duro` → a bare noun "hard"; `verano` → "spring".

### 6.5 Source duplication forced by the hard POS/gender boundary

Several entries repeat identical glosses across blocks, and because rule 1 makes POS and
gender a hard cluster boundary, the partition has to reproduce the duplication with padded
labels. Worst cases: **`naranja`** (four blocks, seven clusters where three would do),
**`parte`** (all five masculine glosses repeated verbatim in the feminine block),
**`mañana`** ("tomorrow" in all three blocks), **`mano`** ("hand" three times in one block),
**`tener`** ("to have" ×3, "to possess" ×2), **`igual`**, **`color`**, **`cabeza`**,
**`seguro`** ("secure" twice), **`volver`** ("to turn" twice), **`padre`** ("father" twice).

### 6.6 Encyclopedia-length glosses

`planta`'s botany gloss is ~60 words on Embryophyta and chloroplasts; `tierra`, `puente`
(ship's bridge, ~40 words), `sopa`, `paz`, `bicicleta` (a football feint described in 45
words), and `planchar` (a 40-word ethnographic note on courting, with broken English —
"in porch") are all unusable as card copy.

### 6.7 Typos and formatting inconsistencies

`torpe` → "clutz" (klutz); `medio` → "arithmatic"; `corto` → an Andalusian coffee described as
"80% milk and 30% coffee" (110 %); `sombrero` → *Clitorea* (should be *Clitoria*);
`perro` → a stray `# ` markup artefact inside the dog gloss; `prueba` → "evidence (usually , in
the plural)"; `paz` → "warlessness" (not an English word); `tigre` → "energic"; `alcanzar` →
"to get to to catch up" (doubled *to*). Missing infinitive `to` on glosses in `curar`,
`elevar`, `entrar`, `escapar`, `estudiar`, `mejorar`, `saltar`, `sacar`, `entender`.

### 6.8 High-value senses missing entirely

`instrumento` (musical instrument), `receta` (medical prescription), `caer` (*caer bien/mal*),
`cuenta` (*darse cuenta*, *tener en cuenta*), `mundo` (*todo el mundo*), `dejar` (to lend),
`afición` (the fans), `cadena` (a chain of shops), `agudo` (the grammatical *palabra aguda*),
`libre` (does **not** mean free of charge — that is *gratis*), `librería` (bookshop, not
library), `dar` (*dar igual*, *dar a*), `valer` (*vale*, *vale la pena*), `duro` (the
five-peseta coin).

  primavera); it may reflect an archaic usage.

## 10. Things a human should double-check

1. **`venir` needs hand-authoring** (§6.1) — it is currently shipping with no definition.
2. The six error-teaching glosses in §6.3 should be removed or corrected before the next
   data deploy; `jugar` → "to play (an instrument)" is the most damaging.
3. **POS and gender were inferred, not read, on the large majority of entries.** ~700 of the
   881 source entries had `pos: null` and `gender: null`. I assigned `v` / `n` / `adj` / `adv`
   and `m` / `f` from the glosses. These assignments are load-bearing (they gate the cluster
   boundary and feed `partsOfSpeech`) and they are my judgement, not the source's. Spot-checking
   a sample of feminine assignments in particular would be worthwhile — `agua`, `alma`, `mano`,
   `día` and `problema` are the classic traps and I flagged each inline, but there will be
   others I got silently wrong.
4. **Author and reviewer were the same process.** Oracle mode makes me both the proposer and
   the strict reviewer of every partition, and all 881 proposals were accepted at review. That
   0 % rejection rate reflects that fact at least as much as it reflects answer quality. The
   clusters are consistent and defensible; they have not been independently checked.
5. Frequency scores are single-judgement estimates of spoken-Spanish frequency with no corpus
   behind them, and several are explicitly regional calls (`frijol`, `tortilla`, `camión`,
   `coger`-adjacent senses) where the "right" score depends on which dialect the app targets.
